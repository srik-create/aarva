For centuries, a heavy, lingering sadness belonged to priests and poets. They treated it as a spiritual trial or the rough price of genius. As this 20 August 2026 essay from Comment Magazine points out, the modern antidepressant changed that, turning despair into a chemical glitch to be fixed. That shift forces a quiet question: if a culture can medicate away its darkest moods, what happens to the meaning behind them? Even as these pills save lives, something is lost when a society stops listening to sorrow and simply tries to erase it.

A history of depression in Western culture, tracing the shift from ancient Greek medicine and early Christian spiritual trials to the modern pharmaceutical model. Placing the current reliance on antidepressants alongside these older traditions shows how medicalizing sorrow changes our understanding of the self. Treating emotional distress strictly as a biological deficit strips sadness of its context, removing the idea that heavy moods might serve a purpose.

The Bulwark ·Mark Hertling

For generations, American soldiers stationed overseas have picked up copies of Stars and Stripes to read about hometown sports and military missteps. But recent firings at the paper force an unusual question: why should the Pentagon fund a publication that openly criticizes its own commanders? The answer in this 25 August 2026 essay has little to do with familiar debates about press freedom. An independent military press actually functions as a tactical necessity—a firewall against the rumors and foreign propaganda that take over the moment troops stop believing their own institutions.

An argument for preserving the editorial independence of Stars and Stripes, the newspaper funded by the Pentagon but historically free from its control. Tracing the publication's mandate back to the Civil War, the author makes the case that uncensored reporting on military problems builds trust among service members. Without a credible source for bad news, the argument concludes, troops will inevitably turn to unverified rumors and foreign propaganda.
