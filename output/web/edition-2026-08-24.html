Cats were domesticated thousands of years ago. Are they finally getting tamer?

In an essay published on 21 August 2026, animal behaviourist Grace Carroll asks a question that seems obvious only in hindsight: are house cats still getting tamer? She looks at how urban foxes and city deer adapt to human handouts, wondering if the modern feline is undergoing a similar biological shift. The answer hinges on a quiet demographic reality. As people have fewer children and live alone longer, society is turning working mousers into surrogate babies. The most dramatic evolutionary change might actually be happening in the owner.

An inquiry into whether cats are still undergoing domestication, using recent studies of urban wildlife as a baseline. Contrasts the self-domestication of cats with the selective breeding of dogs, and links modern pet behavior to falling human birth rates.
