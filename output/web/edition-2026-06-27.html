An investigation into the technical and economic feasibility of carbon capture and storage as a proposed climate solution, assessing the physical requirements for global implementation—including land use, pipeline infrastructure, and storage capacity. How do you build a village for those who have been told they’re someone else’s problem?

Inside Mangav, a unique Maharashtra village for abandoned women and their children

The Hindu

In this piece from 17 June 2026, The Hindu observes a community in Maharashtra that rejects the standard model of the short-term shelter. Mangav operates on the logic that mental health recovery requires more than stabilization; it needs the daily rhythm of a village. By integrating residents into tasks like organic baking and dairy management, the project moves past clinical isolation toward a renewed sense of belonging. The piece considers how a lifelong commitment to dignity might actually function on the ground.

A profile of Mangav, a self-sustaining rehabilitation commune in Maharashtra providing lifelong care for homeless women with mental and physical illnesses. The facility employs a village-based model that integrates psychiatric treatment with occupational therapy through dairy farming, agriculture, and a commercial bakery. Plans for expansion include a 300-bed charity hospital and research center dedicated to mental health and cardiology for underprivileged populations.
