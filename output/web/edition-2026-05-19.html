Humans & Humanity

Why are babies still dying from something a single injection at birth could prevent?

Tiny Footprints, a Blue Blanket: What I Can’t Forget About the Babies Who Died of Vitamin K Deficiency

ProPublica ·Duaa Eldeib

The vitamin K shot is one of the most uncontroversial interventions in newborn medicine — sixty years of standard practice, Nobel Prize-backed science, a single injection at birth. Refusal rates still jumped 77 percent between 2017 and 2024. What's worth sitting with isn't the medical question, which doctors consider settled, but the cultural one: how vaccine skepticism migrated into something that isn't a vaccine, and what that costs — rendered here through autopsy records, which turn out to carry more humanity than you'd expect.

Why Have Immigration Agents Detained This American Citizen Three Times?

ProPublica ·Nicole Foy

The question at the center of this piece isn't really whether immigration enforcement makes mistakes — of course it does. It's what happens when those mistakes are documented on video, go viral, reach Congress, and still keep happening. And what it means when the officials responsible say, on the record, that they haven't happened at all. There's something clarifying about following one person through a system that has decided, repeatedly, that his citizenship is not sufficient proof of his citizenship.

For Your Curiosity

When the government admits it probably got the wrong people, who answers for what it did to them?

Immigrants Detained in Chicago Military-Style Raid Seek Millions in Damages

ProPublica ·Mariam Elba

The September raid on a Chicago apartment complex got saturating coverage — the Black Hawk helicopter, the flash grenades, the TV crew embedded with agents. What this piece follows is the quieter aftermath: the administrative claims filed this week by 17 people detained that night, seeking millions in damages and the more elusive thing that comes before damages, which is an official acknowledgment that something went wrong. The Federal Tort Claims Act is one of the only legal levers available to people harmed by federal agents. Whether it bends is still an open question.
