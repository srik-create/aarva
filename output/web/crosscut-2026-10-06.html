What lingers is the quiet danger of a perfectly smooth screen. A phone makes parting with money completely painless, stripping away the natural, awkward pauses that might make a person stop and think. Putting physical barriers back into the routine—like the simple act of counting paper bills—breaks that trance. But when the rest of daily life trains people to expect instant, invisible transactions, how does someone successfully build a wall around just one dangerous habit?
