Christopher A. Cooper leaves a picture of a man who never stays in one place long enough to be pinned down. Graham’s thirty-year run looks less like a series of contradictions and more like a talent for predicting the weather. He wasn't just following the crowd; he was spotting storms before they even formed. It suggests that survival in a high-stakes environment depends on a total lack of friction with the surrounding world. But then notice the shift in Sally Jenkins’s description of Lionel Messi. On the surface, the soccer pitch and the Senate floor have nothing in common, but the underlying mechanic is the same. Messi spends most of a match just walking, his head swiveling, hunting for the one thing Graham was also chasing: an opening. The interesting thing is the direction of the movement. Where Graham survived by merging with the new majority, Messi survives by finding the "thickets of legs" and moving away from them. He stays relevant at thirty-nine by being "out of the game" until the exact second he needs to be in it. Read alongside Cooper, Jenkins shows that the most effective way to dominate isn't by being the loudest or the fastest, but by being the person who sees the empty space before anyone else realizes it’s there.
