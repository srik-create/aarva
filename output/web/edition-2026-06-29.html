Published on 28 June 2026, this piece examines the tension between the hunger for computing power and the need for fertile land. It asks whether the competition for space is actually a false choice. By looking at agrivoltaics—the practice of farming beneath solar panels—it suggests how a conflict over land might instead become a way to stabilize both food supplies and the grid.

An analysis of agrivoltaics as a dual-use strategy to meet the escalating energy demands of artificial intelligence without displacing food production. Modeling of vertical and tracking solar technologies in the United States and Canada shows how minimal land conversion can offset data-center consumption while improving crop yields. The study also identifies the regulatory barriers currently limiting large-scale implementation in North American agricultural sectors.

The observation here isn't just about culinary tradition, but about the way shared infrastructure creates a specific kind of urban intimacy—one that persists even as the city’s daily habits shift toward the curated world of tourism.

An examination of the culinary and urban history of Marrakesh through tanjia, a slow-cooked meat stew. Interviews with local residents and architectural experts detail how the dish relies on shared neighborhood resources like public bathhouses and communal ovens, tracing its evolution from a working-class staple into a curated symbol of local identity.
