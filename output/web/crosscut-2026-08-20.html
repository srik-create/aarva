What lingers is a collision between hope and arithmetic. There is a very human urge to tame an unforgiving economy by tracking every penny or washing out old Ziploc bags. That kind of extreme budgeting feels like a promise: if you just try hard enough, you will be safe. But then the math simply breaks down, leaving a bankruptcy judge to clear away the wreckage. The question left sitting in the room is this: when the money stops stretching, how much of survival actually comes down to personal discipline, and how much is just luck?
