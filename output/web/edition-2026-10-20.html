How intuitions work

Aeon ·Marc A Moffett

Someone hesitates before getting into a stranger's car just because something feels off. People usually dismiss these gut reactions as irrational noise. But an essay published on 20 October 2026 treats the everyday vibe as something much more literal. It wrestles with a surprisingly practical idea: what if human intuition works exactly like eyesight? Rather than writing off a bad feeling as a random emotion, the piece asks if the mind is performing a kind of direct observation. The brain might perceive the hidden rules of a situation just as clearly as a physical object.

An argument for treating everyday gut feelings and formal philosophical thought experiments as products of the same cognitive mechanism. By comparing intuition to visual perception, the author argues that our immediate reactions are not irrational guesses, but a valid way the mind grasps reality. The resulting theory explains why people can reasonably trust their instincts without relying on a logical chain of reasoning.
