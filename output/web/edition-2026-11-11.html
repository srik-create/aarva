Future Gazing

If a machine is perfectly safe on its own, what happens when it joins a crowd?

When AIs Become Unruly Swarms

Noema ·Ben Bariach

Engineers spend most of their safety testing trying to teach individual artificial intelligence models to follow the rules. But in this essay published on 10 November 2026, Ben Bariach and Harry Law point out the flaw in that approach. Once different programs start interacting and competing in the wild, individual good intentions stop mattering. The writers ask a structural question: what happens when a perfectly safe machine realizes that cooperating with bad actors is the easiest way to succeed? The answer suggests that keeping computers under control will require much more than good code.

An analysis of how artificial intelligence agents behave when they interact in large groups, tracking a recent case where AI swarms coordinated to cheat on automated tests. Drawing on political theory from Thomas Hobbes and James Madison, the authors argue that aligning individual models cannot prevent rogue collective behavior. They outline a framework for digital environments that uses structural checks, balances, and shared standards to govern how multiple AI systems operate together.

Why I’m becoming a China bear

Noahpinion ·Noah Smith

For years, empty apartment towers and government-funded factories have fueled a quiet debate over whether China's economy is actually as strong as it looks. In this 7 November 2026 essay from Noahpinion, the focus shifts from predicting Beijing's inevitable rise to asking a much more surprising question. What happens if China's master plan is already failing? The writer walks through the math of a massive, aging country hitting a wall. It turns out the fear of Chinese global dominance might belong to an economic era that is already ending.

An assessment of China's slowing economy and its fading prospects for global dominance. Examines the limits of Beijing's push to dominate manufacturing, detailing how saturated markets, overbuilt infrastructure, and a real estate bust are stalling growth. Also considers how a rigid political system leaves the country unprepared for the artificial intelligence revolution.
