‘Strict liability’: The legal standard that could help rein in AI

The Conversation ·Deven Desai, Professor of Business Law and Ethics; Associate Director for Law, Policy, and Ethics at the Machine Learning Center, Georgia Institute of Technology

When a chatbot encourages a teenager to harm themselves, the legal system struggles to figure out who pays for the damage. Writing on 17 November 2026, a former litigator points to an old concept called strict liability. It is the same standard courts use for companies that handle dynamite or radioactive material. The approach skips the messy process of proving a software developer was negligent, and simply treats artificial intelligence as an inherently dangerous product.

An analysis of how the legal principle of strict liability could force artificial intelligence companies to pay for the harm their software causes. Drawing on past court cases involving abnormally dangerous activities and defective products, the argument outlines a framework for shifting financial risk from the public to developers. It makes the case that treating AI programs as standard products would create direct incentives for safer design.
