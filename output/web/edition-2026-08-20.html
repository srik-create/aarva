If the ocean keeps getting hotter, how do you build a coral reef that actually survives?

Why scientists are using cheap picnic coolers to protect some of the world’s most endangered animals

Vox ·Benji Jones

For decades, saving a coral reef meant roping it off from fishing boats or trying to rebuild it exactly as it was. But as oceans heat up, replanted corals are dying almost as fast as scientists can glue them to the seafloor. A 17 August 2026 report from Vox captures an uncomfortable shift in how conservationists think about the future. Simply putting nature back is no longer enough. To survive a hotter planet, reefs have to be actively engineered—a massive task that currently relies on store-bought plastic picnic coolers.

Marine scientists in Palau are using store-bought picnic coolers to test the heat tolerance of coral fragments before planting them in the ocean. By exposing samples to high temperatures over a few days, researchers identify which corals resist bleaching and use those survivors to rebuild damaged reefs. This low-cost testing method offers a practical way to keep vulnerable species alive as ocean temperatures rise, despite ongoing debates about its scientific limitations.
