The result is a system that just keeps getting more expensive. Helen Levy, up next, picks up the story exactly where Goldman leaves off. Because the national cost curve never bent, local premiums are still climbing. Levy shows exactly what ordinary people do when they get priced out. A plastic insurance card offers a feeling of safety, even if the policy behind it is hollow. When politicians back away from capping medical prices, that financial pressure doesn't just vanish. It lands squarely on kitchen tables, pushing families toward cheap, unregulated plans that might disappear the moment a real illness strikes. What lingers is how a refusal to make hard choices at the top becomes a dangerous gamble for everyone else. If the political price of controlling hospital bills is too steep, how much longer can ordinary patients afford to carry the risk?
