Why animals are smarter than we think

Vox ·Jonquilyn Hill

Scientists used to put a mark on an animal's forehead and place it in front of a mirror to test its self-awareness. But as philosopher Jeff Sebo points out in this 8 November 2026 interview from Vox, that test only makes sense if the creature actually relies on its eyes. The discussion pauses on a question the scientific community often talks past: why use human habits as the baseline for deciding which species deserve basic respect? Dropping that standard might completely change how society builds its cities and sources its food.

An interview on how humans measure animal intelligence and why traditional tests often miss non-human abilities. The conversation outlines the behavioral evidence for sentience in creatures ranging from bees to livestock, arguing that the capacity to feel pain and pleasure should replace intelligence as the standard for moral concern.
