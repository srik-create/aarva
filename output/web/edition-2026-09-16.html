If a passport is just a travel document, what actually proves you are a citizen?

Explained: Why an Indian passport is not proof of citizenship – and no single document is

Scroll.in ·KBS Sidhu

When India’s foreign ministry confirmed that a passport does not legally prove citizenship, it caused a brief public panic. Writing on 25 June 2026, former civil servant Karan Bir Singh Sidhu explains why lawyers weren't surprised at all. He shows how India quietly dismantled birthright citizenship over the last forty years, replacing it with a strict descent-based system. The insight holds up months later because the underlying problem hasn't gone away. The country still lacks a single document that conclusively proves who belongs, leaving an administrative mess the government still has to untangle.

A historical review of Indian citizenship law, tracing the country's shift from granting citizenship by birth to requiring citizenship by descent. It outlines how three parliamentary amendments narrowed eligibility and created risks of statelessness for children of undocumented migrants, and explains why no single document can conclusively prove Indian citizenship.

How do you perfectly describe the universe when you are standing inside it?

The Myth of Perfect Knowledge

The Atlantic ·Carlo Rovelli

In an 8 September 2026 essay for The Atlantic, a working physicist points out a strange contradiction. For centuries, western thinkers treated the human mind like a camera sitting outside of reality, capturing a perfectly objective picture of the universe. But as quantum mechanics gets better at measuring the physical world, it keeps proving that this detached camera does not exist. Losing perfect objectivity sounds like a scientific defeat, until a quieter possibility emerges. Accepting that absolute truth is a fiction might be the only way to stop treating different perspectives as enemy threats.

An argument against the possibility of pure, objective knowledge, grounded in the limits of modern physics. Uses quantum mechanics to explain how physical properties exist only as relations between things, meaning all human observation is partial and uncertain. Frames this lack of absolute certainty not as a scientific failure, but as a practical case for humility and tolerance.
