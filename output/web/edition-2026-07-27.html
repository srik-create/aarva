How do you update a 250-year-old democracy when the act of voting is no longer enough?

America At 250 & Beyond

Noema ·Nathan Gardels

Writing on 1 July 2026, Nathan Gardels and Nicolas Berggruen look at a mechanical flaw in American self-rule. The country's founders explicitly designed the system to block direct democracy, fearing mob rule. A century later, progressive reformers forced the ballot initiative into law—a tool that corporate interests now routinely spend millions to manipulate. The question now is what a third version of the republic might look like. If representative government is paralyzed and direct voting is easily bought, how exactly do you fix the machinery of public consent?

A proposal for a "third turn" in American governance, building on the Founders' mixed republic and the direct democracy of the Progressive Era. To bypass the special interests that now dominate both systems, it introduces a model called "participation without populism." This approach uses citizens' assemblies and digital platforms to integrate everyday public input directly into the lawmaking process.
