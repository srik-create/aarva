Why do the people who teach literature refuse to say which books are actually good?

Be More Judgmental

The Hedgehog Review ·Hannah Katznelson

In an essay published on 28 October 2026, The Hedgehog Review asks why literature departments are losing their cultural footing right when readers need them most. As language models flood screens with text, people are desperate for someone to tell them what is actually worth their time. Yet academics increasingly refuse to judge literary quality, preferring to justify their work with vague claims about critical thinking. The piece suggests that to survive this era of endless content, scholars need to stop apologizing for their expertise and start doing what they were trained to do: sort good writing from bad.

An argument for using strict standards of literary quality to solve the modern crisis in the humanities. The author compares today's information age to the invention of the printing press, showing how early scholars managed a flood of new books by telling readers exactly what was worth their time. By abandoning these direct judgments for vague claims about critical thinking, modern academics are losing their authority to outside upstarts.
