The Ghanian scholar who changed how the world hears African music

Scroll.in ·Geoff Mapaya, The Conversation

For most of the twentieth century, European music received serious chord analysis, while African music was treated as raw rhythm—something felt rather than thought about. Writing on 10 October 2026, Geoff Mapaya profiles Kofi Agawu, the Ghanaian scholar who broke that double standard. Agawu mastered the rules of Western music theory only to turn them toward the drumming and songs of his native country, proving they carry the same intellectual weight as a classical symphony. The essay pauses on a question that extends far beyond sound: how do you study a tradition without relying on a colonizer's tools?

An examination of how Ghanaian scholar Kofi Agawu shifted the study of African music by applying formal theory to traditions previously dismissed as raw rhythm. Traces his arguments across six books, detailing his case that Western chords acted as a colonizing force and his claim that a steady groove unites African musical thought. Concludes by outlining where other scholars push back against his methods.
