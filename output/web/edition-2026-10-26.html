How to build real peace

Aeon ·Azize Aslan

When civil wars end, fighters are usually told to hand over their weapons and find a job. But pushing them into the exact economic system that sparked the conflict rarely stops the violence. Writing on 26 October 2026, the author asks why standard peace treaties so often fail. By visiting mountain radio stations in Guatemala and community bakeries in northern Syria, they find people trying to build stability from the ground up. The piece pauses on a provocative idea: ending a war might actually require changing how a society works.

An argument that standard peace treaties fail because they leave the capitalist structures that drive violence intact. Why Global Trepanation?

Astral Codex Ten ·Scott Alexander

Up to ten percent of human skulls from the Stone Age feature holes deliberately drilled into them. Even stranger, bone growth patterns show most of these patients survived without modern antibiotics. A 21 October 2026 essay from Astral Codex Ten takes on this archaeological mystery. Why did ancient people across unconnected continents all decide to perform brain surgery with stone tools? The explanation involves high rates of prehistoric violence and a medical misunderstanding that lasted for thousands of years.

A historical and medical explanation for the widespread prehistoric practice of trepanation, or skull-drilling. Early surgeons successfully treated severe head trauma without antibiotics, leading to occasional dramatic recoveries that turned the procedure into a generalized cure-all. The timeline follows the gradual disappearance of the surgery, from a drop in violent injuries during early civilization to the recent collapse of a modern fringe advocacy group.
