Future Gazing

How can the value of a discovery be judged before the language to describe it even exists?

Illegible benefits

Aeon ·Carlo Cordasco

This Aeon essay, published on 30 June 2026, observes a recurring pattern in the history of innovation: the tendency to judge new tools by what they break rather than what they might eventually build. By revisiting the early medical resistance to anesthesia, the piece suggests that the most profound benefits of a technology are often illegible at the start because the concepts needed to describe them do not yet exist. It offers a framework for thinking about AI that prioritizes the idea of recoverability as an alternative to simple restriction.

An analysis of the legibility asymmetry in technological innovation, where immediate costs are measurable but transformative benefits remain conceptually inaccessible until new practices emerge. Historical parallels involving anaesthesia, writing, and the phonograph inform an evaluation of current concerns regarding cognitive degradation from artificial intelligence. Proposes a governance model centered on institutional recoverability to allow for unforeseen advantages while managing known risks.
