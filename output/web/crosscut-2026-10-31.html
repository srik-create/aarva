Where Takhar watches companies invent a new consumer base, Ungar watches the medical establishment try to pull patients back toward actual science. Her reporting focuses on new guidelines encouraging women to return to regulated hormone therapies—treatments that frightened many people away twenty years ago. Put the two together, and a quiet tug-of-war emerges over how to treat a newly visible stage of life. Women are finally demanding relief and refusing to suffer in silence. But read side by side, these writers show a patient caught in the middle. On one side is an unregulated industry happy to sell expensive comforts. On the other is a medical community trying to cut through the marketing noise to offer treatments backed by data.
