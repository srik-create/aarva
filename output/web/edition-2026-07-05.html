When a machine acts as though it has something to lose, how should it be treated?

When The Machines Deserve Our Consideration

Noema ·Grigori Guitchounts

Writing on 2 July 2026, Grigori Guitchounts connects the basement labs of Harvard neuroscience to the silicon architecture of modern AI. The piece moves from a memory of a single lab rat to a proposal for a competence standard—a framework for deciding when a machine warrants moral consideration. It suggests that waiting for a definitive answer on machine consciousness is a mistake. Instead, the focus shifts to observable behavior, asking how a system learns and responds to its environment. It is a pragmatic look at ethical responsibility in the face of irreducible uncertainty.

An analysis of the ethical challenges of extending moral consideration to artificial intelligence, drawing on parallels from laboratory neuroscience and animal research. A proposed competence standard suggests granting moral consideration to AI systems based on integrated cognitive functions like memory, attention, and goal-directed behavior. This framework prioritizes observable capabilities over the unknowable nature of subjective experience to guide the treatment of increasingly autonomous machines.

Maps are powerful political tools shaping a nation’s past, present and future – counter maps allow everyday people to reclaim the narrative

The Conversation ·Patty Heyda, Professor of Urban Design and Architecture, Washington University in St. Louis

Maps often masquerade as objective reality, but this piece from 1 June 2026 suggests they are better understood as instruments of power. From corporate marketing to partisan redistricting, the way lines are drawn dictates who belongs and who is excluded. The focus here is on counter-mapping—a practice where residents use spatial details to reveal the invisible forces, such as economic incentives or infrastructure, that shape their daily lives. It asks what happens when the tools of world-building are placed in different hands.

An examination of cartography as an instrument of institutional power and the role of counter-mapping in challenging dominant geographical narratives. Historical and contemporary examples of map-based exclusion, from corporate marketing to government redlining, are detailed alongside a case study of Ferguson, Missouri, showing how layering disparate data can expose systemic inequalities and provide a framework for the reclamation of urban space.

Maurizio Cattelan’s conceptual artwork Comedian, a banana duct-taped to a wall, serves as a case study in the mechanics of the contemporary art market. Vox ·Dylan Scott

While the technical side of medicine gets the most attention, the relational side is often where the system breaks down. Most people have withheld information from a doctor at some point, usually out of a very human desire to avoid judgment or a lecture. The piece asks what it takes for patients to practice total honesty when the medical system itself often feels like a barrier to trust.

An examination of the psychological and systemic factors that lead a majority of patients to withhold pertinent medical information from their healthcare providers. Drawing on clinical research and expert interviews, it identifies high-stakes disclosures—including medication adherence and substance use—that are critical for effective diagnosis and treatment. It also offers a framework for patients to evaluate the risks of non-disclosure and outlines the physician's role in fostering open communication.
