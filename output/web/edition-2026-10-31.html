How does a thirty-year drug crisis suddenly reverse itself without anyone knowing why?

Opinion: Overdose deaths have plunged. We need to know why

STAT News ·J. Travis Donahoe, David T. Zhu,and Michael L. Barnett

American opioid deaths have dropped by nearly half since 2022. In an essay published on 23 October 2026, three health researchers note that politicians and agencies are rushing to take credit, pointing to border crackdowns and overdose-reversal drugs. But the timeline simply does not add up. The piece pauses on an uncomfortable truth about modern medicine. Experts actually have no idea why the biggest public health turnaround in a generation is happening, mostly because the scientific establishment is looking for answers in the wrong places.

An analysis of the recent drop in American opioid overdose deaths, testing common explanations like border enforcement and naloxone expansion against the data. The authors argue that a shift in the illegal drug supply is the likely cause, but note that researchers lack the data to prove it. They outline how a national survey tracking drug prices and purity could help public health officials understand the decline and prevent future spikes.

But an essay published on 29 October 2026 flips the focus to the quiet utility of giving up. An argument against the cultural obsession with perseverance, making the case for the exploratory value of quitting.
