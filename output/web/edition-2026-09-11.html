When artificial intelligence meets decades of old corporate data, who actually has to clean up the mess?

U.S. companies have an AI problem. Indian IT wants to be the solution

Rest of World ·Itika Sharma Punit

When corporations buy the latest artificial intelligence tools, they usually drop them into outdated, chaotic computer systems. Published on 27 May 2026, Rest of World captures the quiet panic inside American businesses trying to make these models actually turn a profit. The reporting tracks a massive pivot happening in response. Indian IT firms, which have managed the software for Western banks and hospitals for decades, are racing to fix this integration gap. The framing holds up as a clear guide to who stands to profit from the unglamorous labor of making AI useful.

An overview of the Indian IT industry's push to install and manage artificial intelligence for American corporations. The reporting details how firms like Tata Consultancy Services and Infosys plan to use their experience running back-office systems to fix the high failure rate of corporate AI projects. It also outlines the risks of this transition, including direct competition with US consulting firms and the potential loss of their own outsourcing revenue.
