Project Syndicate ·Tom Achoki

When a nation crosses the World Bank’s wealth threshold, the international community celebrates. But this piece from 29 July 2026 points out the quiet disaster that follows the applause. As soon as a country is labeled middle-income, global health aid dries up. The problem is that diseases do not care about gross domestic product. Tom Achoki and Walter O. Ochieng ask what happens when international donors pack up and leave. They find newly promoted countries trapped in a dangerous gap: suddenly too rich to qualify for charity, but still too poor to keep their citizens healthy.

An analysis of the funding gap that opens when developing nations cross the economic threshold from low- to middle-income status. These countries lose access to foreign aid and cheap loans before they can collect enough taxes to fund their own public health systems, leaving them with rising debt and failing medical services. The authors detail how this transition stalls progress and propose new donor rules based on actual disease burdens rather than per-capita income.
