STAT News ·Krutika Kuppalli and Placide Mbala

Writing on 1 July 2026, Krutika Kuppalli and Placide Mbala-Kingebeni examine the friction between rigid diagnostic algorithms and the messy reality of viral outbreaks in Central Africa. The piece suggests that the global health community remains trapped in a reactive cycle, building tools for the last emergency while remaining blind to the current one. By looking at why specific Ebola tests failed to catch a different strain, the authors ask what it would mean to adopt a pathogen-agnostic approach—one that prioritizes a patient’s actual symptoms over a pre-selected list of suspects.

An analysis of diagnostic failures during recent Ebola and Marburg outbreaks in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Uganda, where pathogen-specific tests failed to detect circulating strains. Draws on field experience in outbreak response to propose a shift toward pathogen-agnostic diagnostic systems and syndrome-based testing platforms to identify unknown pathogens before they spread.
