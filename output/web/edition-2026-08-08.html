How out-of-work fishermen saved the American Revolution

The Conversation ·Christopher Magra, Professor of American History, University of Tennessee

Before the American colonies had a formal navy, George Washington faced a massive supply problem. He needed ships, and he needed sailors. Writing in The Conversation on 2 June 2026, a historian frames the origin of the American navy as a massive unforced error by the British. By banning New Englanders from the Atlantic, the Crown accidentally handed the rebellion exactly what it needed. Almost overnight, Washington gained a fleet of salt-stained fishing schooners and thousands of furious, unemployed mariners who were suddenly ready for a fight.

A historical account of how George Washington built America's first naval fleet using unemployed New England fishermen and their converted schooners. After a 1775 British ban on fishing pushed thousands of mariners out of work, Washington armed their vessels to intercept unescorted supply ships off the coast of Boston. By capturing British weapons and provisions, this makeshift navy sustained the Continental Army through the early years of the Revolution.
